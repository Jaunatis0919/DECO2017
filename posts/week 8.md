# Week 8 - Wireframe and Interaction Design

## Part 1: Designing the Garden as the Main Workspace

The wireframe focused first on the Garden page because this is where the user's active emotional posts live. I wanted the page to feel like a working space rather than a landing page. Users should be able to create a burning post, view active posts, and then decide what to do with them.

A key decision was to keep the post actions close to the post itself. Save it and Let it go should not feel like hidden settings. They are the main emotional choices of the app, so they need to be visible and easy to compare.

## Part 2: Meaning of Grave and Ash Field

The Grave area represents keeping a memory. It needed to feel more stable and permanent. The Ash Field represents releasing a post. It needed to feel lighter and more fluid, which is why the dandelion visual became important.

This difference shaped the interaction design. A saved post should appear as a grave card that can be revisited. A released post should become part of a floating Ash Field instead of another normal card. This helps the interface show that the two choices have different emotional meanings.

## Part 3: Visual Tone and Trade-offs

The visual style uses low saturation colours and soft contrast because the content may be emotional. A bright or competitive social media style would not match the purpose of the app. The goal was to make the interface feel quiet, reflective, and personal.

There was a trade-off between symbolic visuals and clarity. Fire, graves, and dandelions can make the experience more memorable, but they can also confuse first-time users if there is not enough feedback. Because of this, the final design needs short labels and clearer transition messages, not only animation.

## Design Decisions

1. I used different visual spaces for active, saved, and released posts because each state has a different emotional meaning.
2. I chose a muted visual style because the app should feel gentle rather than performative or public-facing.

## Personal Reflection

At first, I focused mainly on making the metaphors look interesting. During wireframing, I realised that the emotional metaphor only works if users understand the interaction. The interface has to balance atmosphere with clarity.
